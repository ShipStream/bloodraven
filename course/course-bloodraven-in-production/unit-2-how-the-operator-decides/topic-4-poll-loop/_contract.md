# The poll loop and per-site state

**Unit:** 2 — How the operator decides
**Objectives (unit-numbered):**
1. Walk a site from `unknown` to `writable`, `read-only` or `unreachable` and say what each transition needs   [obj 1]
2. Work out the detection delay from `pollInterval` and `failureThreshold` (2s × 3 = 6s by default)   [obj 2]
3. Explain why `read-only` is entered on a single poll while `writable` needs `recoveryThreshold` successes   [obj 3]

## Topic generation prompt

Open on `playground`, healthy, three sites, the counter application still writing — and the question of what the operator is actually doing in the two seconds between one status read and the next. Teach the loop concretely: `spec.pollInterval` defaults to `2s` (and the operator hard-defaults to 2s when the pointer is nil), every site is probed in parallel under a 5 s per-site `context.WithTimeout` ceiling, and the probe itself is one statement — `SELECT @@read_only`. Teach the four states by their real names and meanings: `StateUnknown`, `StateWritable` (`read_only=0`), `StateReadOnly` (`read_only=1`), `StateUnreachable` (connection failed). Then the debounce, and make the asymmetry the centre of the topic: a connection failure increments `failCount` and only becomes `unreachable` at `failureThreshold` (default 3); a `read_only=1` answer becomes `read-only` on a *single* poll and resets `recoveryCount` to zero; a `read_only=0` answer only becomes `writable` after `recoveryThreshold` (default 2) consecutive successes. Kill the most common wrong derivation explicitly: detection delay is `pollInterval × failureThreshold` = 2 s × 3 = **6 s**, and `recoveryThreshold` is **not a term in that sum** — it gates the opposite transition, back to writable. The asymmetry has one logic behind it: read-only is the safe direction, so it is believed instantly, while writable is the dangerous direction, so it is made to prove itself. Then teach the adaptive poll backoff: once any site's `failCount` exceeds `failureThreshold`, the *whole loop's* interval doubles per extra failure (`interval := base * time.Duration(1<<uint(backoffFails))`), the exponent is capped at `maxPollBackoffExponent = 4`, and there is a 30 s hard cap. Draw the operational consequence the learner is here for: it is driven by the worst-case `failCount` across all sites, so during a sustained outage of one site the loop is polling every 30 s, and a **second** fault on a still-healthy site then takes 30 s × 3 = 90 s to detect rather than 6 s. Show that arithmetic. Finish with the one bypass: a writable observation on a **non-promotable** site skips `recoveryThreshold` entirely, because a writable reader is an immediate safety fact and authority invalidation must not be debounced. Do NOT cover the cross-site evaluation table, what the operator does with these states, fencing mechanics, or cooldown — topics 2 and 3 own those.

## Requested activities

- READ: 900-1100 words. The loop, the probe SQL and its 5 s ceiling, the four states, the two debounce counters, the 6 s derivation with `recoveryThreshold` explicitly excluded from it, the safe-direction/dangerous-direction reasoning, the adaptive backoff with the 90 s second-fault worked example, and the non-promotable bypass. One `flow` widget for a single poll cycle (probe all sites in parallel → apply debounce → compute state → detect transitions). One `compare` widget contrasting the transition *to* `read-only` against the transition *to* `writable` on three questions: how many polls, which counter, and why that direction was chosen.
- FLASHCARDS: 8-10 cards. `pollInterval` 2s, `failureThreshold` 3, `recoveryThreshold` 2, the four state names, the probe SQL, the 5 s probe ceiling, the 6 s detection derivation, the 30 s backoff cap and its exponent cap of 4, the non-promotable bypass.
- QUIZ: 5 questions. Tested discriminations: detection delay computed from `pollInterval × failureThreshold` where the distractors include `recoveryThreshold` in the sum; how many polls a site needs to go `read-only` versus `writable`; what a site's state is after exactly two failed probes with `failureThreshold: 3`; how long a second fault takes to detect while another site has been down for five minutes; whether a writable `read-only`-role site waits for `recoveryThreshold`.

## Handoff

**Inherits:** `playground` is running with three sites and a counter application reading and writing through it; the learner can read its status and describe the current state precisely.
**Leaves:** The learner can take any sequence of probe results for one site and say which state it lands in and after how many seconds, including under backoff.
**Do not cover:** The cross-site table (topic 2), cooldown and failover history (topic 3), logs and metrics (topic 4), anything about what promotion does.
